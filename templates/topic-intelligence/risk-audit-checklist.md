# Risk Audit Checklist

Use before publication and during recurring audits of newly added or materially changed content.

## Source integrity

- Does the primary source resolve?
- Is it actually first-party, regulatory, academic, or otherwise authoritative?
- Does the source support the exact claim being made?
- Are source conflicts disclosed?

## Status language

Do not collapse these states:

- roadmap
- research
- prototype
- demo
- preview
- sample
- pilot
- production-ready
- deployment
- orderable
- generally available

## Commercial claims

Verify or remove unsupported claims involving:

- price
- MOQ
- lead time
- shipment volume
- design win
- named customers
- named partners
- procurement status
- revenue
- ARR
- benchmark leadership

## Performance claims

- Attribute vendor performance figures.
- Do not present vendor benchmarks as independent tests.
- Preserve test conditions where material.

## Copyright and originality

- Write original reporting and analysis.
- Avoid close paraphrase of press releases.
- Use short quotations only when necessary.
- Verify hero/social media rights separately from article-source rights.

## Trademark and affiliation

- Avoid implying endorsement or authorization.
- Use brand names descriptively.
- Do not imitate supplier branding in a way that creates confusion.

## Prediction integrity

- Separate confirmed evidence from interpretation.
- Separate interpretation from prediction.
- Include counter-evidence where relevant.
- Do not increase certainty beyond the evidence.

## Risk classification

### Low

Supported, qualified, and original.

Action: publish or keep public.

### Medium

Important ambiguity remains but can be clearly disclosed.

Action: publish only with explicit attribution and uncertainty.

### High

Material unsupported claim, false commercial implication, provenance problem, rights problem, misleading wording, or copied source language.

Action: hold publication and record the blocker.
